In October I had six projects running at once: six sets of notes, six to-do lists, and six places where I felt behind. I told myself I was keeping everything moving, when really I was just jumping between tabs without giving any of them real attention.

After taking a few years away from building software, coming back made me want to chase every good idea immediately. So I finally forced myself to drop two projects.

SkyBuddy is retired. It was my Chrome extension for Bluesky (keyword glow, negative keywords, and Magic Reply drafts from a local model). I replaced it with XBuddy, which brings those same ideas over to X along with feed rules and six reply tones instead of five.

CopySprout is discontinued. It was the subscription AI writing tool for Etsy sellers with sixteen tools aimed at plain, human-sounding shop copy. Subscriptions ask for steady ongoing attention, and I would rather put that energy into fewer things and make them solid.

Both projects worked and CopySprout had sellers using it, so closing them felt strange at first, though mostly it just freed up room to breathe.

## Six projects is too much context switching

Six projects doesn't sound crazy on paper until each one needs a completely different headspace. CopySprout needed Etsy seller voice, SkyBuddy needed feed rules, Marimo Bot needed core harness architecture, and Moss Toolbox needed business plumbing.

Switching between them every day is where the small details get skipped. You close one tab with a fix half done and open another with a bug half read, and nothing ever gets a proper second pass.

## The trap of fast AI building

AI makes starting a project cheap. I can describe an idea and have a working prototype quickly, which is awesome until the ease of starting tricks you into opening another repo before finishing the last two.

Speed isn't the same thing as care, and I was confusing shipping fast with actually asking what each project was for. Now before opening a new repo I ask three questions: what is this for, who is it for, and is this something I want to put my name on or just something I can knock out today? That third question usually stops me from starting another distraction.

## Focusing on a few at a time

Right now [Marimo Bot](/projects/marimo-bot/) and [Moss Toolbox](/projects/moss-toolbox/) are getting my main attention for second-pass polish.

The biggest difference is simply having enough quiet time to look at what I built last week, spot the chime that plays too loud, fix the empty state that leaves a user guessing, or move a button to where your thumb expects it. Nobody sees your roadmap; they only feel whether the app they opened got a second pass or was rushed out the door.

## Where XBuddy sits right now

XBuddy takes the SkyBuddy idea seriously for X: custom keyword highlight colors, negative keywords to hide noise, up to 20 feed rules based on follower and engagement counts, and six reply tones generated by a local model on my own machine so nothing leaves the browser except calls to my local server.

It isn't on the Chrome Web Store yet, though the source is up on [GitHub](https://github.com/DevTarlow/xbuddy). It works, though X shifts DOM selectors occasionally so I fix them as they break. I built it to help grow on itch and keep my feed focused, and I will lean on it more when I shift back to marketing. Right now the priority is getting the core repos in shape.

There is a longer XBuddy write-up coming, and in the meantime the [XBuddy project page](/projects/x-buddy/) has the details while [SkyBuddy](/projects/skybuddy/) and [CopySprout](/projects/copy-sprout/) stay up as archived project pages.

## If you have too many tabs open

If you are juggling six projects right now, try setting two of them down for a month. Pick the one or two worth a real second pass and give them your full attention before touching anything else.
