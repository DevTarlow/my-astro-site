In October I had six projects going. Six sets of notes, six lists of things still to do, six places to be behind.

I told myself I was keeping them all moving. I was really just visiting them.

I have been away from building for years, and coming back did not make me patient, it made me hungry, and every idea looked like the one that would make the rest easier, so I started it.

I dropped two of them.

SkyBuddy is retired. It was a Chrome extension for Bluesky: keyword glow, negative keywords, Magic Reply drafts from a local model. I replaced it with X Buddy, which does the same work for X with more under it. Feed rules are the new part, and there are six reply tones instead of five.

CopySprout is discontinued. It was the AI writing tool for Etsy sellers, sixteen tools built to sound like a person instead of a brochure, and it was a subscription, which asks for attention every month. I would rather spend that attention on fewer things and make them better.

Both were good projects. CopySprout had sellers using it, and SkyBuddy worked. They were asking for time I had already promised somewhere else.

Closing them felt like putting something down. I expected it to feel like losing something.

## Six is a lot

Six projects does not sound like much on paper. It is a lot when each one needs a different part of your head.

CopySprout needed the seller's voice. SkyBuddy needed the feed's rules. Marimo Bot needed the architecture. Moss Toolbox needed the business. Switching between them was the cost, and I paid it every day.

And the switch is where the details go. You close one tab with a thing half fixed and open another with a thing half read. Nothing gets the second pass.

## The part I got wrong

AI makes building cheap.

I can describe an idea and a working version shows up, which is amazing, and it is also the problem, because when the next project is one prompt away, starting becomes the easiest thing to do. So I kept starting.

And the thing I was not doing was deciding which of them deserved the time.

I read a lot about building fast this year. I wrote some of it. But speed is not the same as attention, and I was confusing the two. I was shipping, and I was not asking what each project was for.

Now I ask a smaller set of questions before I open a repo. What is this for? Who is it for? Is this the version I want to put my name on, or the version I can get done today?

Three questions, and the third one usually tells me to wait.

## A few at a time

[Marimo Bot](/projects/marimo-bot/) and [Moss Toolbox](/projects/moss-toolbox/) are the two that need depth right now. The work that wants a second pass goes there first.

What changed is the amount of looking. On six projects I could give each one a glance. On two I can read what I wrote last week, notice the button that feels wrong, and fix it before the next thing.

A second pass needs a run at the problem, a wrong turn, and enough quiet to notice the wrong turn.

Most of what makes a tool good is in the parts nobody lists as features: the chime that plays at the right volume, the empty state that tells you what to do, the button that is where your thumb expects it. Those are the things I could not see while I was moving between six projects.

With fewer places to look, I find more.

And the person on the other end does not see the roadmap. They see the thing you shipped. If it was rushed, they feel it, even when they cannot name it. Fewer projects means the thing they open has had a second pass.

## Where X Buddy is

X Buddy is the SkyBuddy idea taken more seriously, with keyword highlights in a color you pick, negative keywords that hide what you never want to see, feed rules by follower and engagement counts up to 20 of them, and six reply tones from a model on my machine. Nothing leaves the browser except the call to my own server.

It is not on the Chrome Web Store, and the repo is not public yet. It works, and it is still rough. X changes its site and a selector breaks, so I fix it.

I built it to help me grow on itch and keep my feed aimed at what I want to see. I will use it for real when I switch gears to marketing. Right now the work is the foundation: the repos, the details, and the habit of slowing down enough to see them.

That foundation is the reason I dropped the other two. The attention had to come from somewhere.

There is a longer article on X Buddy coming. For now the [project page](/projects/x-buddy/) has the details, and the [SkyBuddy page](/projects/skybuddy/) is still there as the archive. CopySprout has a [page](/projects/copy-sprout/) too, with the story of how it was built.

## What I would tell you

If you have six projects open, close two of them.

Not forever. Just long enough to look at the other four. The projects that survive the quiet are the ones worth the attention.

What would you keep if you could only keep two? Give that one the next hour. Then give it the one after that.
